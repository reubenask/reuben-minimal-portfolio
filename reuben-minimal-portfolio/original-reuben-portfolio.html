PhD Research
Designing for
Driver Well-being
Zhejiang University · ISE / Mechanical Engineering · 2021 – Present

A multi-framework dissertation measuring what it actually costs a human to operate a smart vehicle across SAE automation levels L0 through L2. The Driver Workload Assessment Framework (DWAF) integrates four measurement dimensions — biomechanical, subjective, physical control, and visual-cognitive.

01
Motion Capture
XSENS · Biomechanics · N=20
+

Used XSENS IMU motion capture suits to track postural adaptation as automation level increased. The question: does the body reflect the cognitive handover to the machine? If the system takes over lateral control, does the human body know?


Findings
  • Elbow movement reduced significantly as automation increased — biomechanical signature of lateral control handover
  • Head velocity and trunk posture showed mixed results — decoupling of visual scanning and physical effort in higher automation modes
  • N=20 pilot, within-subjects, 4 automation modes (L0–L2)
XSENS MTw Awinda IMU Biomechanics Postural Analysis CARLA Simulator
02
Subjective Workload
NASA-TLX · N=51 · Convergent Validity
+

Collected NASA-TLX workload ratings across 51 participants and four automation modes as the external validation anchor — the subjective ground truth against which VWI and ACAI are validated.


Approach
  • 7-point subscale ratings across 6 NASA-TLX dimensions per condition
  • Pairwise comparison sheets for individual subscale weighting
  • Translated to Chinese with careful attention to scientific terminology precision
  • Convergent validity: significant correlation between NASA-TLX and VWI composite scores
  • Serves as external validation for both VWI and ACAI frameworks
NASA-TLX 7-point Scale Pairwise Comparison Convergent Validity N=51
03
Control Authority Index
ACAI · Vehicle Dynamics · TR Part C
+

Solves the attribution problem: when human and machine share control of a vehicle, whose effort produced the outcome? ACAI decomposes physical control workload into Human Effort (HE), Machine Effort (ME), and Performance Quality (PQ) using mode-specific authority matrices.


Key Contributions
  • LoIA null at LKA is structural validation — machine absorbs all lateral input, human contribution approaches zero by design
  • 12-stage Python pipeline implemented end-to-end
  • Human Engagement Index (HEI) in individual- and group-referenced variants
  • First operational framework attributing physical control work to human vs. machine

04
Visual Workload Index
VWI · Oculometrics · Eye-Tracking · N=51
+
51Participants
4Oculometric Signals
3Weighting Schemes

Integrates Fixation Ratio, Visit Rate, Pupil Score, and Blink Rate into a single composite index via person-mean centering and z-score normalisation. Primary discovery: lateral control — not speed — is the driver of visual workload.


Key Findings
  • Two-cluster structure — Manual/ACC high-workload vs. LKA/NOP low-workload, driven by lateral control handover
  • VWI_equal outperforms PCA weighting: PC1 explains only 34.7% of oculometric variance
  • rmcorr C3 (PS × BR) and C4 (VR × FR): r = −0.226, p = 0.015, within-person significant
  • Williams Latin Square counterbalancing; within-subjects design N=51
